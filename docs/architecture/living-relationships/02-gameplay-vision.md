# 02 — Gameplay Vision

## 2.1 Intent

LITTLE SOMEWHERE should feel like a neighborhood where people know one another. Relationships should open doors—literally and figuratively—without turning friendship into a meter to grind or a schedule to manage.

The Living Relationships initiative adds four connected capabilities:

1. **Invitations** — Residents occasionally offer a quiet, specific reason to spend time together.
2. **Home Visits** — The owner (and later a single invited guest) can be present inside an interior when it makes sense.
3. **Teaching** — Residents share knowledge or a ritual tied to their craft. The result is a memory and a new way to participate, not a skill point.
4. **Shared Activities** — Reusable moments (tea, reading, baking, tending, repairing) that two people can do together and that leave a trace.

## 2.2 Player Experience

A typical sequence:

- The player has spoken with Clara several times and helped with a morning bake. The next sunny morning, Clara’s dialogue includes a soft invitation: “If you’re free after the first loaves, the kitchen table is open for tea and a corner of today’s bake.”
- Accepting sets a short-lived invitation. When the player enters the bakery during the window, Clara is present at the table. They share tea. A memory is written. Affinity moves a little. Clara may later mention the morning in passing or to a close neighbor.
- No timer punishes the player for missing it. The invitation simply expires at dusk or the next morning and may be offered again later under similar conditions.

The same pattern applies to Arthur (clock workbench), Mina (greenhouse pots), Nora (reading armchair), and Barnaby (quiet porch or woodshop conversation).

## 2.3 Design Pillars for This Initiative

1. **Curiosity over obligation** — Invitations are offers, never requirements.
2. **Personality over uniformity** — Clara’s invites are warm and food-centered; Arthur’s are rare and precise; Pip’s are spontaneous and outdoor-leaning.
3. **Memory over meters** — The lasting result is a journal entry, an NPC memory, and sometimes a small environmental or dialogue change.
4. **Reuse over new systems** — Prefer day stamps, existing stations, existing dialogue conditions, and existing memory helpers.
5. **Light occupancy** — Start with the owner present at a known station. Do not pathfind multiple NPCs through interiors in early milestones.

## 2.4 Strict Prohibitions

The following are out of scope and must not be introduced:

- Currency, shop prices, or paid friendship.
- Daily login streaks or “missed friendship” penalties.
- Automatic affinity decay for absence.
- Skill trees, XP bars, or mastery levels that gate content behind repetition.
- Forced dialogue or unskippable social scenes.
- Complex calendar micromanagement (the player does not schedule NPCs).
- Turning interiors into management minigames.

## 2.5 Success Criteria

The initiative succeeds when:

- A player who has spent time with a resident can receive and accept a personal invitation.
- Accepting and participating produces a distinct memory and a small, non-grindy relationship movement.
- The resident can be seen inside their own space during the visit window without breaking exterior simulation.
- Teaching unlocks a new ritual or knowledge topic, not a power upgrade.
- The systems remain optional; the existing outdoor neighborhood and free interior entry continue to function exactly as they do today.
